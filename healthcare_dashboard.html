The Data Story

Why this dataset matters and what we set out to discover

🔍
The Central Question
Why do some people visit doctors frequently while others never go? This dataset captures the socioeconomic and health profile of 5,190 Australians to help answer this question systematically.
📋
Data Source
Based on the Australian Health Survey - a widely cited benchmark in health econometrics. Features span demographics, chronic conditions, health scores, and insurance coverage types.
🎯
Analytical Goal
Identify the strongest predictors of doctor visit frequency, uncover demographic disparities in healthcare utilisation, and understand the role insurance plays in access to care.
Doctor Visit Frequency Distribution
Count of 5,190 patients by number of doctor visits (0-9)
Heavily Right-Skewed Distribution: 79.8% of patients have zero visits. This classic count-data distribution means standard linear regression is inappropriate. Poisson or Negative Binomial regression are the correct model choices for this target variable.
Gender Split & Visit Rate Comparison
Patient gender distribution and average visits per group
Female patients visit 53% more often (0.362 vs 0.236). This aligns with global patterns - women seek medical care more proactively, including preventive, reproductive, and wellness services.

Insurance Paradoxes - Three Counterintuitive Findings
🔷 Private Insurance Paradox: Private holders (0.295) visit slightly less than uninsured (0.307). This is the "healthy subscriber" effect - wealthier, healthier individuals can afford private cover and also need less care. Cost is not the primary barrier for this group.
🔴 Freepoor Underutilisation (0.158): Despite free coverage, low-income patients visit the least. This signals non-financial barriers: transport access, work schedule constraints, healthcare literacy gaps, or social stigma around seeking care.
🟢 Freerepat High Utilisation (0.467): Veterans with free government care visit 81% more often than average. Veterans carry higher chronic condition burdens from military service - and free care removes all financial barriers, enabling full utilisation.

📖 The Complete Data Story
Across 5,190 Australians, healthcare utilisation is not random - it follows clear, predictable patterns rooted in three pillars: illness burden, demographic profile, and healthcare access structure.

The data opens with a striking fact: nearly 80% of people never saw a doctor in the survey period. This zero-inflation is the defining statistical feature and means any predictive model must account for the two-stage decision process - first, whether to visit at all, then how many times.

When illness does strike, the frequency escalates sharply. Illness count is the #1 predictor, and reduced activity days multiply that signal into a reliable severity index. Chronic conditions compound the picture: a limiting chronic condition alone doubles expected visits, while non-limiting conditions add only a modest 27% increase - confirming that functional impairment is what truly drives healthcare-seeking behaviour.

Demographics tell their own story. Women seek care proactively, seniors accumulate health needs over time, and lower-income patients face a double burden - greater disease exposure and greater dependence on public systems. The most striking paradox is the freepoor underutilisation: the group with the greatest healthcare need uses it the least, a clear signal that financial barriers are not the only obstacle - structural and social barriers also prevent vulnerable populations from accessing the care that is already available to them.

For analysts and policymakers, the prescription is clear: model visit counts with Poisson or Negative Binomial regression, invest in outreach for low-income groups, expand chronic disease management, and plan senior healthcare capacity proactively. The data has spoken - the next step is action.
